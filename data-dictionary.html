The data dictionary

72 entities · 142 typed relations · 18 pages · 215 internal edges · generated 31 August 2026

Every page on this site publishes a structured-data graph. This is the model behind it, written out in full: which entities exist, how they relate, in which direction, and how many of each. It is generated from the graph that is actually served — not written alongside it — so it cannot drift out of date without the site changing too.

The machine-readable form is at /data-dictionary.json. The vocabulary is the glossary; the measured signals are the 167 signals.

1. Notation

Each relation is a triple: subject → property → object. Cardinality is read from the subject towards the object.

NotationMeaning
1..1The subject points at exactly one object through this property.
1..NThe subject points at one or more objects through this property.
↑ inboundThe same relation seen from the object end. Every relation below is listed from both sides.
inverseThe property that names the reverse direction. Where schema.org defines an inverseOf we name it; where it does not, the reverse is real but unnamed, and we say so rather than invent a term.

5. Why publish this

A structured-data graph is a set of claims about how a business is shaped. Most sites emit one and never look at it again, which is how a graph ends up asserting that a page is about an organisation that no longer exists, or that an offer covers a service that was renamed. The dictionary is the readable form of the claim, so the drift is visible.

Publishing it also does something for machines: a system reading this site does not have to reverse-engineer our model from fifteen separate pages. The relations, their directions and their cardinalities are stated once, in one place, in a form it can fetch.

It is generated, not written. If the graph changes and the dictionary does not, that is a bug in our build, and it is the kind of bug our own engine would flag.
