EU AI Act Transparency

Last updated: 25 August 2026 · Operator: AIVENTURE S.R.L., Romania

This page sets out how 3webobs.com positions itself in relation to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — the European Artificial Intelligence Act — and what we disclose about the AI components of the Service.

Summary. The Service uses AI for one narrowly defined purpose: writing a short narrative summary of an already-computed technical result. Scores themselves are produced by deterministic rules applied to publicly observable evidence, not generated by a language model.

1. Where AI is used, and where it is not

ComponentAI involved?How it works
Fetching the website and its signal filesNoStandard HTTP requests
Detecting structured data, schema, crawler directivesNoDeterministic parsing
Scoring each signalNoFixed rules against observed evidence; unverifiable signals are marked not applicable
Action plan orderingNoSorted by rule weight of failed signals
Written executive synthesisYesA general-purpose language model turns the computed result into a short narrative

This separation is deliberate. It means the numbers you see are reproducible — the same website in the same state produces the same result — and that no score is invented by a model.

2. Transparency — Article 50

Article 50 of the AI Act requires that people be informed when they are interacting with AI-generated content. Accordingly:

  • Where a written synthesis is displayed, it is generated by an AI system operating on the audit result.
  • Numerical scores, signal verdicts and evidence descriptions are not AI-generated.
  • You may request an explanation of how any individual signal verdict was reached.

3. Risk classification

Our assessment is that the Service does not fall within the prohibited practices of Article 5, and does not constitute a high-risk AI system under Annex III. It does not perform biometric identification, emotion recognition, social scoring, or evaluation of natural persons for access to education, employment, credit, essential services or law enforcement purposes.

The Service evaluates the technical configuration of a website. It does not profile individuals and does not produce decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on a person.

Where a language model is used for the narrative summary, the Service acts as a deployer of a general-purpose AI model supplied by a third party, and the transparency obligations above apply.

4. Human oversight

Reports are diagnostic outputs, not automated decisions. Every recommendation is intended to be reviewed and acted upon by a human. We do not present the report as a compliance verdict, a certification, or a substitute for professional advice.

5. Data governance

  • Inputs are publicly accessible website resources.
  • No personal data is deliberately used to train any model.
  • Content sent to the AI provider for synthesis is limited to the computed report; it is not used by us to build training datasets.
  • Where a signal cannot be verified, it is reported as not applicable rather than assigned a fabricated value.

6. Accuracy and limitations

The report reflects what was publicly observable at the moment of the scan. It may be affected by temporary outages, geographic content variation, security challenges on the target website, JavaScript-rendered content not present in the served HTML, and the absence of external data sources not connected to a given deployment.

Nothing in a report should be read as an assertion that a third party is or is not compliant with any legal obligation. A report identifies observable technical signals; it does not deliver a legal conclusion.

7. Timeline

The AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and applies in stages. Prohibitions and AI literacy obligations applied from February 2025; obligations for general-purpose AI models from August 2025; the general body of obligations, including transparency duties, applies from August 2026, with certain high-risk provisions phased in later.
