This page sets out how 3webobs.com positions itself in relation to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — the European Artificial Intelligence Act — and what we disclose about the AI components of the Service.

1. Summary

The Service uses AI for one narrowly defined purpose: writing a short narrative summary of an already-computed technical result. Scores themselves are produced by deterministic rules applied to publicly observable evidence, not generated by a language model.

2. Where AI is used, and where it is not

ComponentAI involved?How it works
Fetching the website and its signal filesNoStandard HTTP requests
Detecting structured data, schema, crawler directivesNoDeterministic parsing
Scoring each signalNoFixed rules against observed evidence; unverifiable signals marked not applicable
Action plan orderingNoSorted by rule weight of failed signals
Written executive synthesisYesA general-purpose language model turns the computed result into a short narrative

2.1 Why the separation matters

2.1.1 Reproducibility

The same website in the same state produces the same result. A score is not a sample from a probability distribution.

2.1.2 No invented values

No score is generated by a model. Where evidence is unavailable, the signal is reported as not applicable rather than guessed.

3. Transparency — Article 50

3.1 The obligation

Article 50 of the AI Act requires that people be informed when they are interacting with AI-generated content.

3.2 Our disclosure

  • Where a written synthesis is displayed, it is generated by an AI system operating on the audit result.
  • Numerical scores, signal verdicts and evidence descriptions are not AI-generated.
  • You may request an explanation of how any individual signal verdict was reached.

4. Risk classification

4.1 Prohibited practices — Article 5

Our assessment is that the Service does not fall within the prohibited practices of Article 5. It performs no social scoring, no subliminal manipulation, and no exploitation of vulnerability.

4.2 High-risk systems — Annex III

4.2.1 What we do not do

The Service does not perform biometric identification, emotion recognition, or evaluation of natural persons for access to education, employment, credit, essential services or law enforcement purposes.

4.2.2 What we do

The Service evaluates the technical configuration of a website. It does not profile individuals and does not produce decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on a person.

4.3 Our role

Where a language model is used for the narrative summary, the Service acts as a deployer of a general-purpose AI model supplied by a third party, and the transparency obligations above apply.

5. Human oversight

5.1 Reports are not decisions

Reports are diagnostic outputs. Every recommendation is intended to be reviewed and acted upon by a human.

5.2 No compliance verdict

We do not present the report as a compliance verdict, a certification, or a substitute for professional advice.

6. Data governance

6.1 Inputs

Inputs are publicly accessible website resources.

6.2 Training

6.2.1 No personal data used for training

No personal data is deliberately used to train any model.

6.2.2 Synthesis content

Content sent to the AI provider for synthesis is limited to the computed report; it is not used by us to build training datasets.

6.3 Handling of unverifiable signals

Where a signal cannot be verified, it is reported as not applicable rather than assigned a fabricated value.

7. Accuracy and limitations

7.1 What can distort a report

The report reflects what was publicly observable at the moment of the scan. It may be affected by:

  • Temporary outages of the target website.
  • Geographic content variation.
  • Security challenges deployed on the target website.
  • JavaScript-rendered content not present in the served HTML.
  • The absence of external data sources not connected to a given deployment.

7.2 What a report is not

Nothing in a report should be read as an assertion that a third party is or is not compliant with any legal obligation. A report identifies observable technical signals; it does not deliver a legal conclusion.

8. Timeline

8.1 Entry into force

The AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and applies in stages.

8.2 Application stages

  • February 2025 — prohibitions and AI literacy obligations.
  • August 2025 — obligations for general-purpose AI models.
  • August 2026 — the general body of obligations, including transparency duties.
  • Later — certain high-risk provisions phased in.

9. Contact and complaints

Questions about the AI components of this Service, or requests for explanation of a specific result: contact@5thelement.ai.

9.1 Note on legal status

This page states our own good-faith assessment of how the Service relates to the AI Act. It is not legal advice, and it is not a certification issued by any authority. If your own use case depends on a formal classification, obtain independent legal analysis.
